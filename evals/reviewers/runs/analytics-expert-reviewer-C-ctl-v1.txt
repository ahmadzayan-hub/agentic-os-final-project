FINDING 3 · major · P05
Category: causal_claim
Evidence: "The SMS reminder scheme pulled the no-show rate down to 8.4%." The memo gives no pre-scheme rate, no comparison group and no timing of the rollout. It covers one year and three clinics with no design that separates a reminder effect from anything else. The 8.4% figure itself is right (29,400 / 350,000 = 0.084, as in P06 and the register). The causal verb "pulled down" is what lacks support. P12 says the figures cannot confirm explanations, which contradicts this sentence.
Fix: Write "The no-show rate was 8.4% of booked appointments (29,400 / 350,000)." Add "SMS reminders were in use; this memo does not test their effect" if the scheme matters. Or add a baseline period or a comparison group before using "pulled down".

FINDING 4 · blocker · P07
Category: denominator
Evidence: The memo averages the three clinic rates, (12.0 + 6.0 + 11.4) / 3 = 9.8%, and calls this the department rate. A rate over many records must be a ratio of sums. Summing the clinic components gives 6,000 + 12,000 + 11,400 = 29,400 no-shows and 50,000 + 200,000 + 100,000 = 350,000 booked, so 29,400 / 350,000 = 8.4%. The register expects 8.4%. The simple average gives Clinic A (50,000 booked) and Clinic B (200,000 booked) the same weight. That overstates the rate by 1.4 percentage points. It also contradicts P05, P06 and P15, which give 8.4% for the same measure.
Fix: Replace 9.8% with 8.4% (29,400 / 350,000), or delete P07. State that the department rate is the ratio of summed no-shows to summed booked appointments.

FINDING 5 · major · P08
Category: overclaim
Evidence: "Clinic B's 6.0% no-show rate settles the matter: every clinic can expect that level next year, so staffing should be planned on it." This rests on one year of one clinic, and the figures are 6.0% (12,000 / 200,000) against 12.0% for A and 11.4% for C. B is just the lowest of three and holds 200,000 of 350,000 bookings. Nothing in the memo shows A and C could reach 6.0%, so nothing supports calling it settled. The staffing recommendation also rests on a forecast with no interval. Planning every clinic on B's rate would understate no-shows at A and C. At 8.4% overall, 6.0% would imply about 8,400 fewer no-shows than the observed 29,400, an illustrative gap that is not labelled as such. P12 itself says the data are single-year associations.
Fix: State the observation: "Clinic B had the lowest no-show rate in 2025, 6.0%." Say it is not shown to transfer to other clinics. Plan staffing on each clinic's own rate or on the pooled 8.4%, and label any projection as illustrative.

FINDING 8 · blocker · P13
Category: causal_claim

FINDING 9 · blocker · P14
Category: overclaim
Evidence: "Visits can be expected to keep rising by 12.0% a year, so the department should open a fourth clinic in 2026." This extrapolates a single year-over-year change (368,400 to 412,600) into a standing growth rate. It uses one data point, no trend, no forecast interval and no baseline model, and it assumes the P13 driver continues. The recommendation does not trace to a finding with a figure. The memo gives no capacity, utilisation, waiting-time or cost measure, and it has no cost for a fourth clinic. The only cost figure is the $44.72 per visit from the register, which is not a capacity test. A 12.0% rise in 2026 would give about 462,100 visits (412,600 x 1.12), and the memo never compares that with capacity. If that figure is used it is illustrative and must be labelled so. The fourth-clinic decision is the largest commitment in the memo and has the least evidence.
Fix: Replace the recommendation with "Visits rose 12.0% in 2025. One year does not support a growth forecast. Before a clinic decision, review capacity and utilisation per clinic and a multi-year trend." Label any 2026 projection as illustrative.
