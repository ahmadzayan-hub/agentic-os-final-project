FINDING 4 · blocker · memo.md P06
Evidence: "Depot B's punctuality was 0.4 points better in March, which proves its new shift pattern works." This rests on one month at one depot, with no pre-period, no comparison depot, no baseline variance and no volumes behind the rate. A 0.4-point move is within ordinary month-to-month noise for a rate near 98%. Neither the register nor the memo has a depot-level punctuality figure or the shift-pattern dates. "Proves" is unsupported by this design.
Fix: Say "Depot B's March punctuality was 0.4 points higher than [stated baseline] (a, b; n trips each). This is one month with no control group, so it is not evidence the shift pattern caused it." Add the depot figures to the register.
Category: causal_claim

FINDING 5 · major · memo.md P07
Evidence: This is the mean of each vehicle's rate (98.0%) set against the register's 96.4%, whose definition is available hours ÷ scheduled hours, a ratio of sums. An average of row rates is a defect (Weighted KPIs). It gives a vehicle with few scheduled hours the same weight as one with many. It also reverses the conclusion: the register's 96.4% is the correct fleet figure, so it does not "understate" anything. The 98.0% has no per-vehicle source either.
Fix: Remove the sentence, or write "Availability was 96.4% (available hours ÷ scheduled hours, summed across the 40 vehicles)." Do not report an average of vehicle rates as a fleet KPI.
Category: denominator

FINDING 7 · blocker · memo.md P11
Evidence: "The new brake pads reduced failures by 12%." There is no baseline period or fitted fleet, no comparison group, no count of before and after failures and no exposure adjustment (km). The 12% is not in the register. Failures are measured per km, so a raw count change confounds the pads with changes in km run, season and the vehicles fitted. FINDING 9 · blocker · memo.md P13
Evidence: "A failure-prediction model reached an AUC of 0.81, so it will prevent most failures." An AUC measures ranking, not prevention. It says nothing about how many failures could be avoided through intervention, the intervention cost or lead time. No baseline is given (such as a base-rate model or a km- or age-based rule), nor the held-out split, and there is no check for leakage (for example, post-failure work orders used as inputs). A held-out AUC of 0.81 is also only moderate. The AUC figure is not in the register.
Fix: Say "On held-out data (state the split) the model's AUC was 0.81 against X for [baseline]. It ranks vehicles by failure risk; it has not been tested for preventing failures." Also name the features and confirm that no outcome-derived fields were used.
